Find Bite

Find Bite is an affordable dining app, made by a student for students. It brings discounted food from restaurants, grocery stores and surplus inventory into one place, so college students can eat well on a budget while helping reduce food waste.

01 — Research

User research

I interviewed three college students about how they eat day to day, what they weigh when buying food, how they feel about food waste and how they use existing food apps.

01

Price & value

Participants prioritized affordability and were willing to compromise on appearance when food offered better value.

02

Food waste

Participants were concerned about food waste and were open to buying surplus food that would otherwise be discarded.

03

Existing services

Participants found existing food apps limited by availability, a lack of discounts and added delivery costs.

02 — Define

User persona

Goals
Find affordable, good-quality food while maintaining a healthy and sustainable lifestyle.
Motivations
Save money on food so she can still enjoy eating out, try new foods and spend her limited budget elsewhere.
Pain points
She has a limited food budget, doesn't know where to find good discounts, and finds existing delivery apps too expensive.
Design opportunity

How can we help college students discover affordable food while reducing food waste?

03 — Journey

Understanding the user journey

Mapping how students currently find cheap food showed three stages, each with its own point of friction.

01

Discover

Users search various apps and websites for affordable food.

! Deals are often scattered or outdated.

02

Evaluate

Users compare price, availability, location and pickup time.

! Deals may sell out before the user arrives.

03

Pick up

Users travel to pick up the food and judge its quality.

! The food may not meet her expectations.

04 — Opportunities

Design opportunities

01

Centralized discovery

Bring discounted food into one place instead of requiring users to search multiple platforms.

02

Reduce uncertainty

Show availability and pickup information before users make the trip.

03

Speed up discovery

Use location, preferences and filters to help users find relevant deals quickly.

How might we

Make affordable surplus food easier to discover and more reliable to purchase?

05 — Structure

Information architecture

The app's structure follows the journey: sign in, browse nearby deals by location, filter by price, food type, availability and distance, check a deal's details, then check out and receive a QR code for pickup.

Early design

I translated the structure into a low-fidelity user flow: log in, browse, filter, view a deal, check out and pick up. Users browse nearby food in list or map view, filter and compare deals, then reserve one and receive pickup details.

06 — Final design

The app

Landing page

Welcome

The landing screen introduces the brand and leads straight into logging in or signing up.

Log in / sign up

Quick access

Users can sign in or create an account with email, or continue with Google or Facebook, so getting started takes only a moment.

Discovering deals

Location-based discovery

Find Bite brings discounted food from restaurants, grocery stores and surplus inventory into one place.

The home experience prioritizes nearby options and organizes them by food source, helping users quickly scan and compare available deals.

Exploring nearby

Map-based discovery

Because discounted food requires physical pickup, proximity is an important part of deciding whether a deal is worthwhile.

The map view lets users explore nearby food options geographically and search within their local area.

Evaluating deals

Deal details

Once users choose a food provider, they can browse available discounted items, compare prices and build their order.

Surprise bags list ingredients and allergens, along with past reviews.

Cart

Building an order

The cart gives users an overview of their selected items before moving into checkout, with clear prices, an order summary and an obvious next step.

Check out

Checkout with context

Since Find Bite requires physical pickup, checkout communicates more than payment. Users review their order alongside a map of the vendor, its exact location and an estimated pickup time before committing.

Order confirmed

Pickup confirmation

After checkout, the experience shifts from purchasing to completing the physical pickup, with a QR code to show at the store.

07 — Testing

Usability testing

I tested the prototype with 3 participants, each completing 3 tasks.

01 — Discovery needed more clarity
Participants found it unclear how the different types of deals were organized.
02 — Location wasn't visible enough
One participant couldn't easily tell how close the stores were from the home page.
03 — Search + map caused confusion
Participants had difficulty understanding the relationship between search/browse and the map view.
04 — Checkout was straightforward
Participants were able to add items and proceed through checkout with little difficulty.
